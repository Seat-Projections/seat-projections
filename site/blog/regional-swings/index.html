What if Victoria doesn't swing evenly? Seven seats get a new favourite

Midyear polling puts the Greens on stronger ground in Melbourne's west than in our forecast, but on weaker ground in the south. What would that mean in November?

20 November 2026 · 2 min read

Key points

  • The Coalition averages 51.7 seats instead of 52.3. Its chance of a majority falls from 79% to 77%.
  • Labor averages 18.7 seats instead of 18.1. No party moves by a full seat on average.

Take Albert Park and Footscray. Both YouGov and RedBridge found less support for the Greens in Southern Metropolitan, which includes Albert Park, than our forecast suggests. In Western Metropolitan, which includes Footscray, they found more. If that pattern has held since midyear, the same statewide Greens vote could produce quite different contests in the two seats.

To test that idea, we used the two firms' MRP surveys, which estimate district support from statewide polling. We gave them equal weight and compared their estimates across Victoria's eight upper house regions, keeping the November statewide polling targets, candidates and preference assumptions unchanged.

The surveys date from June and July, so we moved each region only part of the way towards the midyear pattern: about 30% on average across 5,000 simulations. That weight is a judgement call. We varied it by party and region and allowed extra uncertainty rather than assuming the pattern had held everywhere.

Where the favourite changes

The table shows the two candidates with the highest chance of winning in each version. These are win chances, not vote shares. In Oakleigh and Tarneit, just one percentage point separates the leaders under the regional adjustment.

Footscray needs a little more care. Jess Marsh's chance of winning falls from 37% to 23%, partly because the adjustment reduces the regional vote for “Others”, which includes independents. That's not evidence that Marsh herself has lost support.

Seven changes of favourite don't mean seven seats have changed hands. The Liberals are better placed in Albert Park and Oakleigh, while the Coalition loses ground in Bendigo East and Tarneit. Those shifts largely offset each other statewide. The published forecast is unchanged: this comparison shows which local calls depend most on where the votes are.
