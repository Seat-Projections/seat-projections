Final Call: Coalition majority with 55 seats

Our final forecast gives the Coalition an 87.7% chance of winning at least 45 of Victoria's 88 lower-house seats on Saturday 28 November.

26 November 2026 · 2 min read

We expect the Liberals and Nationals to govern in their own right after Saturday's Victorian election. Across 5,000 simulations they average 55.4 seats: 46.1 Liberal and 9.3 National. The headline rounds that average to 55. It doesn't mean we've identified 55 certain Coalition wins.

The final numbers

PartyAverage seatsMiddle 80% of simulations
Coalition55.443 to 67
Labor15.45 to 27
One Nation8.72 to 16
Greens8.15 to 11
Independents0.40 to 1

The majority line is 45 seats. It's inside the Coalition's range: in about one simulation in eight, the Liberals and Nationals fall short.

What changed in the final week?

New polls from RedBridge/Accent, Roy Morgan and Resolve took our polling inputs through 23 November. Since our 20 November forecast, the Coalition has risen from 31.3% to 32.2% in our first-preference average, while Labor has slipped from 21.9% to 21.3%. With the candidate and preference assumptions unchanged, the Coalition gained three seats on average, up from 52.4. Labor fell from 18.3 to 15.4.

One Nation finishes just ahead of Labor in our polling average, on 21.8%, but well behind it on expected seats. Votes are counted district by district. A strong third-place finish can lift a party's statewide vote without winning it a seat. In Point Cook, for example, One Nation was our previous favourite. The Liberals now have the highest chance of winning, at just 39%. It's a change of favourite, not a safe Liberal gain.
