Methodology · 03

Model 2 — core-candidate heuristic

The 2026 forecast model. A preferential count only has a few decisions that change the winner — model 2 resolves those decisions explicitly, thousands of times.

Model 2 splits each seat’s declared 2026 candidates into core — the top four of the pre-preference totals in at least 5% of runs, plus credible independents — and minor. Every minor vote is sent straight to the core along per-party flow vectors, then the core contest is resolved explicitly for whichever exclusion order occurs.

Model 2 specification

primary[seat, party] = baseline_2022[seat, party] + statewide_swing[party] (one draw per run, shared by all seats) + regional_deviation[region, party] + seat_noise[seat, party] (re-normalised to sum to 100% per seat) core total T[k] = primary[k] + Σ_m primary[m] · f[m → k] core stage: exclude the lowest core candidate; their pile splits between the continuing candidates by each vote’s party of origin (origin tracking); repeat until two remain — the higher wins.

Statewide swing is centred on the polling average with spread from historical polling error; regional deviation captures uneven swings; seat noise covers candidate effects and retirements and is much wider for credible independents. All noise widths are placeholder values — see the caveat on the projections page.

Origin tracking

A core candidate’s pile contains votes inherited from minor candidates. Each pile is kept broken down by the party its votes started with, so when the candidate is excluded each part transfers by its own party’s preferences — Greens-origin votes sitting with One Nation still go mostly to Labor.

Splitting the whole pile by the excluded candidate’s own split was dropped after the B1 replays.

One Nation & independents

One Nation barely contested the 2022 Lower House (8,077 votes statewide), so its 2026 geographic pattern comes from model 1’s federal-booth replay rather than the state baseline. Independents are core only when flagged credible — high-profile, recontesting, or strong in 2022 — and carry much wider seat noise.

Polling

How the polling average is built

Model 2 uses a deliberately simple average: each pollster’s latest eligible poll within 35 days of the newest poll in the register, equally weighted. There is no house-effect adjustment, no exponential decay and no sample-size weighting. The register is maintained from electiontracker.au.
