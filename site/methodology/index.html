How it fits together

One pipeline

Every projection on this site is produced by the same pipeline: votes flow into districts, districts are counted by an exact preferential count, and Monte Carlo simulation turns point estimates into probabilities. The count engine, benchmarks and datasets are documented on this page; each forecast model has its own page — linked from the Methodology menu above.

The count engine

An exact preferential count, not a swing model

Every seat projection is a full instant-runoff count reproducing the count an electoral commission would run.

Exact instant-runoff count

Each district is counted by eliminating the lowest-polling candidate and distributing their ballots to the next continuing preference, one exclusion at a time. Two modes mirror the two published datasets: official stops once a candidate reaches the majority threshold ⌊formal⌋/2 + 1; full-2CP continues until two candidates remain.

Ties are broken by the statutory lookback rule — Electoral Act 2002 (Vic) s 114(5): the candidate behind at the previous stage is excluded first, with further lookback or lot-drawing if the tie persists.

Ballot decomposition

Commissions publish each count as a round-by-round distribution of preferences, not raw ballots. The engine deterministically decomposes those tables into exact integer ballot bundles — sets of preference orders with weights — that reproduce every round’s transfer row and progressive total with zero vote error.

Verified vote-for-vote against all 88 VEC 2022 distributions and the 48 published 2018 counts.

Geography

Booth-to-district mapping

Federal polling places are assigned to state districts geometrically: a booth counts only if it sits inside the district and within 1 km of its own federal division.

EBC 2021 redivision boundaries · AEC polling-place locations
Preferences

Fitted preference flows

How each party’s ballots split between remaining candidates is fitted from published distributions of preferences — for benchmarks, leaving out the scored district so nothing is fitted on its own result.

VEC, AEC and other state commissions’ published distributions
Uncertainty

Monte Carlo seat simulation

Each simulation redraws statewide swing, regional deviations, per-seat noise and preference flows, then counts all 88 districts — turning point estimates into probabilities and seat ranges.

The models

Two different questions

Baseline Model — “nothing changes”

Every polling place’s votes from the most recent federal election are put into the state district it sits in, and each district’s preferential count is replayed assuming nothing changes — a deliberately primitive floor the real model must beat.

Read the methodology →

Forecast Model — core-candidate heuristic

Declared 2026 candidates are split into core and minor; minor votes flow to the core and the core contest is resolved by origin tracking over whichever exclusion order occurs, against the 2026 polling average.

Backtests

Benchmark results

Both models are scored against electoral commissions’ declared winners across eight real elections — but the two rows mean different things.

B1 replays vs forecasts

Forecast Model rows are B1 replays: the model is given each election’s actual primaries and flows fitted without the scored district, isolating the preference model and count. Baseline Model rows are genuine forecasts from federal votes — the two are not comparable.

The metrics

Brier: for each seat, add up (p − o)² over the candidates — p the probability given, o 1 or 0 — averaged across seats; 0 is perfect. Log loss: the average of −ln(p) for the actual winner, floored at p = 0.001 (a cost of 6.9 for that seat).

The raw seat count only looks at the favourite; Brier and log loss also reward calibrated confidence — a 55% call that loses costs little, a 99% call that loses costs a lot.

Reproduce the numbers

Every archived forecast ships a reproduce.sh that downloads the inputs (cached under data/) and reruns the models; seed and settings are in each run’s run_metadata.json. AEC input files carry SHA-256 checksums in data/federal/*/manifest.json.

Step-by-step commands are in the repository README, Reproducing the results.

Independence charter

Non-partisanship & citation

Seat Projections is an independent civic data project. It accepts no donations, has no party affiliation and no advertising; every number on this site is produced by open code from published electoral data, so any result can be recomputed and challenged.

Corrections and methodology questions are welcome via the repository’s issue tracker or email hello@seatprojections.au.
