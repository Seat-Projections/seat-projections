Methodology · 03

Forecast Model — core-candidate heuristic

The 2026 projection. A preferential count only has a few decisions that change the winner — the Forecast Model resolves those decisions explicitly, thousands of times.

Called model2 in the code and archived runs.

The Forecast Model splits each seat’s declared 2026 candidates into core — the top four of the pre-preference totals in at least 5% of runs, plus credible independents — and minor. Every minor vote is sent straight to the core along per-party flow vectors, then the core contest is resolved explicitly for whichever exclusion order occurs.

Forecast model specification

primary[seat, party] = baseline_2022[seat, party] + statewide_swing[party] (one draw per run, shared by all seats) + regional_deviation[region, party] + seat_noise[seat, party] (re-normalised to sum to 100% per seat) core total T[k] = primary[k] + Σ_m primary[m] · f[m → k] core stage: exclude the lowest core candidate; their pile splits between the continuing candidates by each vote’s party of origin (origin tracking); repeat until two remain — the higher wins.

Statewide swing is centred on the polling average with spread from historical polling error; regional deviation captures uneven swings; seat noise covers candidate effects and retirements and is much wider for credible independents. All noise widths are placeholder values — see the caveat on the projections page.

Origin tracking

A core candidate’s pile contains votes inherited from minor candidates. Each pile is kept broken down by the party its votes started with, so when the candidate is excluded each part transfers by its own party’s preferences — Greens-origin votes sitting with One Nation still go mostly to Labor.

Splitting the whole pile by the excluded candidate’s own split was dropped after the B1 replays.

One Nation & independents

One Nation barely contested the 2022 Lower House (8,077 votes statewide), so its 2026 geographic pattern comes from the Baseline Model’s federal-booth replay rather than the state baseline. Independents are core only when flagged credible — high-profile, recontesting, or strong in 2022 — and carry much wider seat noise.

Polling

How the polling average is built

The Forecast Model uses a deliberately simple average: each pollster’s latest eligible poll within 35 days of the newest poll in the register, equally weighted. There is no house-effect adjustment, no exponential decay and no sample-size weighting. The register is maintained from electiontracker.au.

Forecast history

Forecast history

Snapshots of the forecast are taken every two weeks from 1 September 2026, skipping any date whose polling average would be identical to the previous kept snapshot’s (so the cadence stretches to roughly monthly when few polls are published). A snapshot uses only polls whose fieldwork ended at least two days before the snapshot date, as a proxy for publication.

The backfilled snapshots up to 10 November 2026 are retrospective: they were generated on 16 November 2026 with the final candidate fields and current model settings, so they show what the model would have said, not forecasts published on those dates. Snapshots made from now on are frozen when generated.

Drag the timeline under the forecast map on the projections page: the marks are the model runs above, and the map blends seat probabilities between them (cards and figures always show a real run, not a blend). Open a snapshot directly, e.g. /#model2@2026-10-13. Reproduce with run_model2.py --snapshots or --as-of YYYY-MM-DD.
